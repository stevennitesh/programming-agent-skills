# Company-Type Model Selection

Choose methods by business economics. Do not treat this table as a substitute
for inspecting the issuer.

| Company type | Primary methods | Key drivers | Avoid or qualify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mature non-financial | FCFF DCF, reverse DCF, EV/EBIT or P/E cross-check | volume, price, margins, ROIC, reinvestment, leverage | extrapolating one-off margins or working capital |
| High-growth profitable | Multi-stage FCFF, reverse DCF, controlled forward multiples | market size, share, unit economics, margin path, reinvestment efficiency | fixed five-year fade with no competitive or capacity logic |
| Loss-making or pre-profit | Revenue-to-normalized-FCFF DCF, reverse DCF, EV/Sales cross-check | survival, funding, unit economics, break-even scale, dilution | P/E, PEG, or EBITDA before the denominator is meaningful |
| Bank or lender | Residual-income/excess-return model, DDM, justified P/B or P/TBV | asset quality, net interest margin, fees, credit losses, capital, ROE, cost of equity | FCFF, treating deposits as ordinary debt, EV/EBITDA |
| Insurer | Residual-income/excess-return model or DDM, P/B with sustainable ROE | underwriting, reserves, combined ratio, float, investment returns, capital | industrial-company WACC and EBITDA |
| REIT or property company | NAV, AFFO/FFO multiples, DDM or property DCF | rent, occupancy, lease terms, same-store NOI, cap rates, recurring capex, leverage | GAAP EPS/P/E as the sole metric |
| Regulated utility or infrastructure | FCFF, DDM, rate-base or asset valuation | rate base, allowed and earned ROE, capex recovery, load, regulation, leverage | unsupported perpetual premium growth or ignoring regulatory lag |
| Commodity or cyclical | Mid-cycle DCF, normalized earnings, NAV or asset value | cycle-normal price/volume, cost curve, reserves, capacity, balance sheet | peak/trough P/E and straight-line recent growth |
| Multi-segment firm | SOTP when supportable; coherent consolidated and reverse valuation | segment economics, central costs, taxes, claims, capital allocation | one blended peer multiple for unlike segments |
| Pre-revenue biotech | Probability-adjusted NPV, cash/runway and dilution scenarios | trial phases, probabilities, timing, addressable patients, pricing, costs | P/E, ordinary terminal-growth DCF |
| Resource exploration | Risked NAV, scenario and funding analysis | reserves/resources, recovery, commodity curve, capex, permitting, ownership | revenue multiples without asset and funding economics |
| Distressed firm | Probability-weighted going concern and liquidation/recovery | liquidity, maturities, covenants, refinancing, seniority, dilution | single going-concern DCF |
| Declining or runoff | Finite-life DCF, liquidation or runoff value | decline rate, harvest margin, obligations, asset sale proceeds | perpetual growth unsupported by economics |
| Holding company | Look-through NAV or SOTP | ownership percentages, taxes, debt, central costs, liquidity discounts | consolidated earnings multiple without look-through |

When multiple company-type rows apply, map the material economic components,
not only reported segments, and admit the level of analysis by evidence. Use
SOTP only when component inputs and the segment-to-parent bridge are
supportable, including intersegment eliminations, shared assets or funding,
central costs, taxes, and common claims. Use a consolidated method only when
its value or cash-flow base, claim bridge, and required return are coherent at
the consolidated level and not materially distorted by incompatible component
economics. Do not invent component inputs or unsupported proxy allocations. If
neither route is supportable, apply the main skill's bounded-partial or
dependent-method block policy; any bound covers the omitted component's full
positive and negative effect, including interactions, taxes, and claims.

## Mixed Operating-And-Financial Economics

Apply this branch when an issuer otherwise routed as non-financial, including a
multi-segment issuer, has material lending, lease-financing,
custody/customer-funds, insurance-underwriting, or asset-linked funding
activity. Map the material related assets, liabilities, cash flows, and funding
sources to the operating business, financial activity, or customer claim.
Identify assets unavailable to the target security and the liabilities or
restrictions that encumber them.

Apply the tie-breaker above to select a consolidated method, SOTP, or safe
failure.

Do not count restricted or customer funds as excess cash without evidence of
availability, subtract activity-linked funding as corporate debt while also
valuing its funded economics as operating, or classify originations and
repayments as industrial working capital without a consistent stated treatment.

## Selection Questions

1. Is debt financing or an operating input?
2. Are earnings, cash flow, book value, or assets the economically meaningful
   claim base?
3. Is the firm profitable, reinvesting, transitioning, cyclical, or distressed?
4. Can revenue, margins, reinvestment, and risk be forecast independently?
5. Are multiple segments governed by different economics?
6. Does regulation constrain capital, payout, pricing, or growth?
7. Is value dominated by binary events, separable assets, or finite reserves?
8. Do options, convertibles, ADRs, or different share-class rights alter the
   claim being valued?

Use at least one intrinsic or asset-based method when supportable and one
market-pricing cross-check. A reverse DCF is useful whenever a current market
price exists. Omit a method whose denominator or assumptions are not meaningful
and explain why.

## Sector-Specific Metrics

Use sector metrics only when they connect to cash economics:

- SaaS: ARR/revenue growth, net revenue retention, gross margin, sales
  efficiency, churn, SBC and dilution, path to free cash flow.
- Retail: same-store sales, units, traffic, ticket, gross margin, inventory,
  lease obligations, store-level returns.
- Semiconductors: end-market mix, inventory cycle, utilization, pricing,
  capital intensity, foundry commitments, mid-cycle margins.
- Industrials: backlog quality, book-to-bill, price/cost, utilization, working
  capital, maintenance and growth capex.
- Energy and mining: production, decline, reserves, realized price, unit cost,
  sustaining capex, commodity sensitivity, decommissioning obligations.
- Banks: loan/deposit growth, deposit beta and mix, NIM, charge-offs, reserves,
  tangible book, regulatory capital.
- Insurers: premium growth, combined ratio, reserve development, investment
  yield, capital adequacy.
- REITs: same-store NOI, occupancy, lease expiry, tenant concentration, AFFO,
  recurring capex, debt maturity, implied cap rate.
- Utilities and infrastructure: rate-base growth, allowed versus earned return,
  regulatory lag, recoverable capex, load or contracted volume, and debt service.
- Marketplaces and platforms: gross transaction value, take rate, cohort
  retention, contribution margin, incentives, concentration, and network
  liquidity.
- Biotech: asset-by-asset probability, phase transition, enrollment, approval
  timing, addressable population, net price, patent life, cash runway.

Do not award a premium merely for a fashionable sector metric. Translate it
into duration, margin, reinvestment, cash flow, dilution, or risk.
