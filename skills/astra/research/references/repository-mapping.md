# Repository mapping

Read when researching how a requirement, definition, method, or named behavior
maps through a target artifact or repository, whether internally or externally
defined, or the historical reason for code.

Pin the exact revision or captured working state. Trace the complete local chain
needed for the claim: inputs, formulas/code, configuration and precedence,
transformations, outputs, tests, and observed behavior. Include generators,
overrides, policies, decisions, or rendered artifacts where they affect that
chain. Record inspected identities and missing links; reread mutable decisive
surfaces before concluding. On drift, preserve unaffected findings and reconcile
the affected mapping rather than constructing a hybrid state.

Distinguish static alignment, material difference, and unresolved mapping from
observed runtime behavior and empirical effectiveness. Static correspondence
proves neither execution nor quality in practice. Existing test source establishes
what it asserts; it is not evidence that the test passed on this candidate.
Report the sourced concept, observed local expression, discrepancy, and only
consequences justified by the evidence. Recommendations may be requested, but
research itself does not silently implement or claim experimental validation.

Code establishes mechanics, not the author's motivation. For historical intent,
inspect contemporaneous decisions, commits, reviews, issues, or comments; otherwise
label the inference and plausible alternatives. Stated motivation establishes
what was recorded, not proof that the change achieved its intended effect.
