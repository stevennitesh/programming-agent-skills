# Writing Great Skills Glossary

Load only when choosing or defining a leading word, changing invocation or
reference-loading behavior, splitting a skill, promoting a lesson across
skills, or specifying derived state.

## Behavior language

**Leading word:** a familiar term or short phrase—such as TDD, YAGNI, seam,
tracer bullet, or vertical slice—that carries a coherent body of practice. It
earns its place when the intended meaning fits and the term removes more
explanation than it creates.

## Invocation

**Implicitly invocable:** Codex may discover the skill from its description; a
human may still name it explicitly.

**Explicit-only:** semantic auto-invocation is disabled. A human must name the
skill or approve one exact caller-owned invocation packet that names it;
otherwise callers recommend it and stop.

**Description:** the routing predicate for an implicitly invocable skill. Name
observable entry triggers and the closest exclusions without runtime procedure
or a body summary.

## Information hierarchy

**Branch-only reference:** keep common behavior inline. Put branch-only
material behind a pointer that names both its condition and target. When
required branch material is missed, sharpen that pointer first; inline the
material only if the precise pointer still fails.

**Skill split:** split only for independent invocation, irreducible branch
load, or an observed early stop after sharpening the completion criterion.

## Transfer and state

**Transfer gate:** promote a lesson beyond one skill only at the narrowest
shared owner. Name the behavior class, observable applicability condition, one
materially different applicable case, and the closest non-applicable case.

**Derived view:** treat it as a projection of its owning facts unless it has
independent behavior. Reuse the fact owner and update boundary instead of
creating another ledger, state machine, or proof loop. Update owning facts and
required projections as one prospective mutation, then validate their agreement
before publication.
