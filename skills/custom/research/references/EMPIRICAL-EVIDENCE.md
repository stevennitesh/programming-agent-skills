# Empirical Evidence

Load this branch when a claim concerns effectiveness, causality, reliability,
generalization, or a body of observed evidence. Otherwise do not load it.

Define the population, intervention or exposure, comparison, outcome, setting,
and time horizon that the claim actually needs. For an individual result, judge
whether the design, sampling, measurement, missing data, analysis, and control
of confounding can support that claim. Distinguish association from causation.

For a body of evidence, judge study limitations, independence, consistency,
directness, precision, and selective or missing results. Report effect magnitude
and material uncertainty rather than treating statistical significance as the
answer. An original study owns its result; a sound synthesis may own an
aggregate conclusion.

Use a systematic-review protocol only when the caller requests one or the
claimed breadth requires exhaustive identification. Under that trigger,
predefine eligibility, searched channels and queries, dates, screening and
extraction rules, and the synthesis method; return or link that method record.
Otherwise apply these checks proportionally and preserve the exact empirical
limit.
